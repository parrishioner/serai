## EXAMPLE CANDIDATE PROFILE: Growth PM (B2B SaaS)
##
## This is a working example for a senior PM with 10+ years in B2B SaaS,
## spanning platform/ecosystem, growth, and core product. Edit the
## dimensions, weights, anchors, and disqualifiers to match your own
## priorities. The system evaluates whatever you put here — nothing is
## hardcoded in the prompt.


=== ROLE ===

Senior Product Manager — Growth, Platform, or Core Product
B2B SaaS companies building products for technical or business users
10+ years of experience across platform infrastructure, developer ecosystems,
growth/monetization, and core user-facing product

Strongest fit: companies where PM owns outcomes end-to-end (not feature
factories), experimentation is a core muscle, and the product has real
distribution or data advantages — not just a good idea.


=== WHAT I BRING ===

- Growth & monetization: owned onboarding, activation, and conversion for
  8M+ MAU product. Ran experiments that generated $3M+ incremental ARR.
- Platform & ecosystem: built developer data transfer platforms, led
  ecosystem migrations (server-to-cloud), managed 25K+ developer ecosystem
- Experimentation: Amplitude, Databricks, Statsig. Comfortable designing,
  running, and interpreting experiments at scale.
- Cross-functional leadership: partnered across engineering, security, legal,
  and developer relations on complex platform changes (GDPR, API deprecation,
  auth remediation)
- Builder instincts: built this tool (Serai) to solve my own job search
  problem. Comfortable with Python, APIs, and LLM integration.


=== COMPANY EVALUATION DIMENSIONS ===

moat_durability (weight: 0.25)
  What to evaluate: Does this company have structural advantages that
  compound over time? Look for distribution channels, network effects,
  proprietary data, switching costs, or ecosystem lock-in. Brand and
  market share alone are not moats — they erode without structural
  reinforcement.
  9-10: elite distribution + data moat with strong ecosystem lock-in
        (Atlassian, Stripe)
  7-8:  strong product-led distribution, growing switching costs
        (Ramp, Linear)
  5-6:  moderate moat, contestable position; includes AI model companies
        where the core product (model intelligence) is actively contested
        by 3+ well-funded competitors (Anthropic, OpenAI)
  3-4:  weak or eroding moat, low switching costs (ZoomInfo)
  1-2:  no meaningful moat, easily replicated (Jasper)

growth_trajectory (weight: 0.20)
  What to evaluate: Is revenue accelerating or decelerating? Look for
  funding rounds, ARR milestones, customer growth, and market expansion
  signals. Distinguish between genuine growth and growth funded by
  unsustainable burn.
  9-10: triple-digit revenue growth, breakout trajectory, strong unit
        economics or clear path to them (Harvey, Ramp)
  7-8:  strong double-digit growth with clear market tailwinds
        (Anthropic, Stripe, Clay)
  5-6:  moderate growth, stable but not accelerating (Zapier, Atlassian)
  3-4:  stagnation or deceleration, flat or declining metrics
        (ZoomInfo, Jasper)
  1-2:  declining revenue, distressed fundamentals

product_culture (weight: 0.20)
  What to evaluate: Can a PM actually own outcomes here? Look for evidence
  of a named CPO or strong product leader, PM hiring patterns, product-led
  launches, experimentation infrastructure, and PM-authored public content.
  Absence of evidence is a LOW score, not a medium score. Do not infer
  product culture from company success.
  9-10: PM owns outcomes, experimentation is a core muscle, strong CPO,
        product-led decision making (Linear, Stripe)
  7-8:  good PM function, some evidence of ownership and experimentation
        (Ramp)
  5-6:  PM function exists but may be secondary to engineering or sales
  3-4:  sales-driven or engineering-driven, PM is a project manager in
        practice
  1-2:  no meaningful PM function, feature factory or founder-driven

ai_leverage (weight: 0.15)
  What to evaluate: Is AI a genuine product differentiator or just
  marketing? Look for AI-native products, proprietary models or training
  data, AI-driven workflows, or meaningful automation. Distinguish between
  "uses AI" and "AI is the product."
  9-10: AI-native product with proprietary model, data, or workflow layer
        (Harvey, Anthropic, OpenAI)
  7-8:  AI meaningfully enhances the core product (Ramp, Stripe, Clay)
  5-6:  some AI integration, but not core to the value proposition
  3-4:  AI is marketing, not product
  1-2:  no AI strategy or actively resisting AI adoption

leadership_quality (weight: 0.10)
  What to evaluate: Is the executive team credible, stable, and making good
  decisions? Look for founder credibility, executive hires, board quality,
  and recent leadership changes (positive or negative). Layoffs alone do
  not indicate bad leadership — context matters.
  9-10: exceptional founder + executive team with strong track record
  7-8:  credible leadership with mostly positive signals
  5-6:  mixed signals — some strengths, some concerns
  3-4:  leadership instability, questionable decisions, or recent
        executive turnover raising concerns
  1-2:  leadership crisis, founder conflicts, or board dysfunction

market_category (weight: 0.10)
  What to evaluate: Is the category itself growing? Look for TAM expansion,
  regulatory tailwinds, secular trends, and competitive density. A great
  company in a shrinking category is a worse bet than a good company in an
  expanding one.
  9-10: large and expanding category with strong secular tailwinds
        (AI infrastructure, fintech, legal tech)
  7-8:  healthy category with room for multiple winners
        (developer tools, sales automation)
  5-6:  mature category, still growing but competitive
        (project management, iPaaS)
  3-4:  crowded or commoditizing category (CRM, data brokerage)
  1-2:  shrinking or dying category


=== ANCHOR COMPANIES ===

The anchor companies above define what each score means for each dimension.
When evaluating a new company, compare it against these anchors. If a company
listed as an anchor appears in the evaluation, its score MUST fall within the
stated band for that dimension.

Note: anchor placement reflects dimension-specific assessment, not overall
company quality. A company can be an elite anchor for moat (9-10) and a weak
anchor for product culture (3-4) simultaneously.


=== CANDIDATE-SPECIFIC DISQUALIFIERS ===

If a company matches any of these categories, flag it — but do NOT let the
flag affect dimensional scores. Score every dimension on its own evidence.
Disqualifier routing is handled in code after scoring.

- Consumer social / gaming — company's PRIMARY business is consumer-facing
  social or gaming. Companies that have some consumer users but are
  primarily B2B do NOT match this disqualifier.

- Legacy enterprise sales org — large, established enterprise company where
  the sales organization drives product decisions, PM has no meaningful
  ownership, and the company has been in this mode for 10+ years. Growth-
  stage companies with enterprise sales motions do NOT match this
  disqualifier. A company scoring 5+ on product_culture is unlikely to
  match.

- Hardware — company whose primary business is manufacturing physical
  hardware, chips, or semiconductors. Software companies that sell to
  hardware companies do NOT match.


=== HARD CONSTRAINTS ===

Location: San Jose, CA or Remote
Level: Senior, Staff, Principal, Group PM
Minimum base compensation: $200,000


=== ROLE EVALUATION CRITERIA ===

Title preferences (in order):
1. Senior Product Manager, Product Manager (direct match)
2. Growth PM, Platform PM, Core PM (specialty match)
3. Staff PM, Principal PM, Group PM (level match)
4. Director of Product (adjacent-up, evaluate carefully)
5. Technical Program Manager (adjacent only if PM-track scope)

Positive role signals:
- Explicit experimentation or A/B testing in the role description
- Growth, monetization, or activation ownership
- Platform or infrastructure product scope
- Developer ecosystem or API product scope
- Direct mention of PM ownership of outcomes or metrics
- Cross-functional leadership across engineering + design + data

Negative role signals:
- "Project manager" scope disguised as product manager title
- Pure process or program management without product ownership
- Heavily regulated domain with no room for experimentation
- Role requires deep domain expertise I don't have (e.g., healthcare
  compliance, financial trading systems)
- "VP of Product" at a <50 person company (likely founder-PM, not IC PM)
