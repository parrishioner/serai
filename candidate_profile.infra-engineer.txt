## EXAMPLE CANDIDATE PROFILE: Senior Infrastructure Engineer
##
## This profile demonstrates how a different role uses different dimensions
## and weights. Compare with candidate_profile.growth-pm.txt — same system,

=== ROLE ===
 
Senior Infrastructure Engineer / Staff Engineer
Companies building systems at meaningful scale — distributed systems,
platform infrastructure, developer tooling, or data infrastructure
8+ years of experience across backend systems, cloud infrastructure,
reliability engineering, and platform teams
 
Strongest fit: companies where engineers own the full lifecycle of their
systems, infrastructure is a product (not a cost center), and technical
decisions are made by the people closest to the code.
 
 
=== WHAT I BRING ===
 
- Distributed systems: designed and operated services handling 100K+ RPS
  with sub-100ms latency requirements
- Platform engineering: built internal developer platforms (CI/CD, deploy
  tooling, service mesh) used by 200+ engineers
- Cloud infrastructure: deep AWS experience (EKS, DynamoDB, SQS, Lambda),
  infrastructure-as-code (Terraform, Pulumi)
- Reliability: on-call culture, SLO-driven development, incident response
  frameworks, chaos engineering
- Technical leadership: led architecture reviews, mentored senior engineers,
  drove RFC processes for cross-team technical decisions
 
 
=== COMPANY EVALUATION DIMENSIONS ===
 
engineering_depth (weight: 0.30)
  What to evaluate: Does this company solve genuinely hard technical
  problems? Look for published engineering blog posts, open-source
  contributions, conference talks, infrastructure scale, and evidence
  of custom-built systems vs. off-the-shelf solutions. Companies that
  outsource core infrastructure or rely entirely on managed services
  score lower.
  9-10: pushing the boundaries of distributed systems, publishing novel
        work (Stripe, Cloudflare, Databricks)
  7-8:  serious infrastructure challenges at real scale
        (Ramp, Linear, Figma)
  5-6:  standard cloud-native stack, competent but not exceptional
  3-4:  minimal engineering complexity, CRUD applications at moderate scale
  1-2:  no meaningful technical challenges
 
moat_durability (weight: 0.20)
  What to evaluate: Does this company have structural advantages that
  compound over time? For infra companies, look especially for data moats,
  network effects, protocol-level lock-in, and ecosystem ownership.
  9-10: deep infrastructure moat — protocol-level lock-in, massive data
        advantage, or ecosystem ownership (Stripe, Cloudflare, Databricks)
  7-8:  strong technical moat with growing switching costs (Datadog, Figma)
  5-6:  moderate moat, competitive but defensible position
  3-4:  weak moat, commoditizing product
  1-2:  no meaningful differentiation
 
growth_trajectory (weight: 0.20)
  What to evaluate: Is this company scaling in a way that creates
  interesting engineering problems? Rapid growth means new challenges —
  scaling systems, hiring, and evolving architecture. Stagnation means
  maintenance mode.
  9-10: hypergrowth creating novel scaling challenges
        (Anthropic, Harvey)
  7-8:  strong growth with clear infrastructure scaling needs
        (Ramp, Databricks)
  5-6:  steady growth, stable but not creating new technical challenges
  3-4:  flat or declining, likely in maintenance mode
  1-2:  shrinking, cutting engineering headcount
 
ai_leverage (weight: 0.15)
  What to evaluate: Is AI creating interesting infrastructure challenges?
  Look for ML training infrastructure, inference optimization, data
  pipelines at scale, or novel AI-native architectures. "Uses AI" is
  not the same as "AI creates hard infrastructure problems."
  9-10: AI IS the infrastructure challenge — training, inference, data
        pipelines at frontier scale (Anthropic, OpenAI, Databricks)
  7-8:  AI meaningfully shapes infrastructure decisions (Harvey, Ramp)
  5-6:  some AI workloads but not core infrastructure challenge
  3-4:  AI is a feature, not an infrastructure concern
  1-2:  no AI relevance
 
leadership_quality (weight: 0.10)
  What to evaluate: Does engineering leadership have credibility? Look for
  technical founders, respected CTOs/VPs of Engineering, and evidence that
  leadership understands infrastructure investment as a long-term bet, not
  a cost to minimize.
  9-10: exceptional technical leadership with strong eng culture
  7-8:  credible technical leaders, engineering has a seat at the table
  5-6:  mixed — some good technical leaders, some concerns
  3-4:  non-technical leadership making infrastructure decisions
  1-2:  engineering is a cost center, not a strategic function
 
market_category (weight: 0.05)
  What to evaluate: Is the category itself growing in a way that sustains
  infrastructure investment?
  9-10: massive and expanding (cloud infrastructure, AI, fintech)
  7-8:  healthy growth, infrastructure matters (developer tools, security)
  5-6:  mature but stable
  3-4:  commoditizing, infrastructure investment declining
  1-2:  shrinking category
 
 
=== ANCHOR COMPANIES ===
 
The anchor companies above define what each score means for each dimension.
When evaluating a new company, compare it against these anchors. If a company
listed as an anchor appears in the evaluation, its score MUST fall within the
stated band for that dimension.
 
 
=== CANDIDATE-SPECIFIC DISQUALIFIERS ===
 
If a company matches any of these categories, flag it — but do NOT let the
flag affect dimensional scores. Score every dimension on its own evidence.
Disqualifier routing is handled in code after scoring.
 
- Consultancy / agency — company's primary business is building software
  for other companies, not owning and operating its own products.
 
- Legacy enterprise — large, established company where infrastructure
  decisions are driven by procurement and vendor relationships rather than
  engineering judgment. The platform hasn't been meaningfully rearchitected
  in 5+ years.
 
- Consumer social / gaming — company's primary business is consumer-facing
  social or gaming. Infrastructure may be interesting but the product
  domain is a mismatch.
 
 
=== HARD CONSTRAINTS ===
 
Location: San Francisco Bay Area or Remote
Level: Senior Engineer, Staff Engineer, Principal Engineer
Minimum base compensation: $220,000
 
 
=== ROLE EVALUATION CRITERIA ===
 
Title preferences (in order):
1. Senior Infrastructure Engineer, Staff Infrastructure Engineer
2. Senior Platform Engineer, Staff Platform Engineer
3. Senior Backend Engineer (if infrastructure-focused)
4. Site Reliability Engineer / SRE (if building, not just operating)
5. Principal Engineer (architecture scope)
 
Positive role signals:
- Distributed systems or large-scale data processing mentioned
- Ownership of production systems end-to-end (build + operate)
- Internal platform or developer experience scope
- Open-source contribution expected or encouraged
- Mentions of Kubernetes, Terraform, or similar infrastructure tooling
  as things you'd improve, not just use
- Small team with outsized impact (not a cog in a 500-person eng org)
 
Negative role signals:
- "DevOps" title with no software engineering expectations
- Pure operations / on-call without building new systems
- Maintaining legacy monoliths with no modernization mandate
- Vendor management disguised as engineering
- Requires specific domain expertise I lack (e.g., embedded systems,
  kernel development, hardware firmware)
