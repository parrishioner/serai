## EXAMPLE CANDIDATE PROFILE: Senior Growth Product Manager
##
## This profile demonstrates how a growth-focused PM configures Serai.
## Compare with candidate_profile.infra-engineer.txt — same system,
## different lens. Edit everything below to match your own priorities.


=== ROLE ===

Senior Product Manager / Staff Product Manager — Growth & Monetization
B2B SaaS companies where product-led growth matters — self-serve funnels,
trial optimization, onboarding, activation, and conversion.
7+ years of experience across growth, experimentation, and product
management at scale.

Strongest fit: companies where PMs own outcomes (not tickets), growth is
a strategic function (not a marketing team side project), and there's real
experimentation infrastructure to run and learn from tests.


=== WHAT I BRING ===

- Growth systems: designed onboarding, trial conversion, and activation
  flows for products with 1M+ monthly signups
- Experimentabuilt and ran A/B testing programs using Amplitude,
  Statsig, or LaunchDarkly — comfortable with statistical rigor and
  interpreting ambiguous results
- Monetization: owned pricing and packaging experiments, free-to-paid
  conversion, and expansion revenue strategy
- Platform thinking: experience with multi-sided marketplaces, developer
  ecosystems, and integration platforms
- Cross-functional leadership: worked across engineering, design, data
  science, marketing, and sales to ship growth initiatives


=== COMPANY EVALUATION DIMENSIONS ===

moat_durability (weight: 0.25)
  What to evaluate: Would this company survive if competitors could
  replicate its features overnight? Moats that count: distribution,
  proprietary data, network effects, workflow depth, brand, regulatory.
  Weak moats: generic horizontal productivity, thin AI wrappers.
  9-10: deep structural moat — network effects, data advantage, or
        ecosystem lock-in (Atlassian, Figma, Stripe)
  7-8:  strong product moat with growing shing costs (Notion, Linear)
  5-6:  moderate moat, competitive but defensible
  3-4:  weak moat, easily replicated feature set
  1-2:  no meaningful differentiation

growth_trajectory (weight: 0.20)
  What to evaluate: Revenue growth, user growth, product shipping
  velocity, funding trajectory. Consistent compounding beats spiky.
  9-10: hypergrowth — 3x+ YoY, category-defining momentum
  7-8:  strong growth — ~2x YoY, hiring aggressively, shipping fast
  5-6:  steady but plateauing — selective hiring, mature product
  3-4:  flat or declining — hiring freeze, stagnation
  1-2:  shrinking — layoffs, revenue decline 50%+

product_culture (weight: 0.20)
  What to evaluate: Does PM get to do PM work? Look for strong CPO or
  product-first founder, PMs owning outcomes, writing culture, real
  experimentation infrastructure. Do NOT infer product culture from
  revenue or company success — look for direct evidence.
  9-10: exceptional PM function — outcomes-driven, strong experimentation
        cul real authority (Linear, Stripe, Figma)
  7-8:  healthy PM org — product-first leadership, PMs influence strategy
  5-6:  mixed — some product influence, but sales or eng often overrides
  3-4:  sales-led — PMs write specs for sales requests
  1-2:  no distinct PM function

ai_leverage (weight: 0.15)
  What to evaluate: Is AI a meaningful force multiplier on the product,
  or just a buzzword? Look for AI that improves the core product
  experience, not bolt-on features.
  9-10: AI-native product in a specialized domain (Harvey, Anthropic)
  7-8:  AI meaningfully enhances the core product (Ramp, Notion AI)
  5-6:  credible AI roadmap but not yet shipped
  3-4:  AI in the pitch deck, not the product
  1-2:  no AI strategy

leadership_quality (weight: 0.10)
  What to evaluate: Founders with prior exits or recognized product
  taste. Credible exec hires. Low churn. Consistency between public
  statements and actions.
  9-10: exceptional founder-leaders with strong product vision
  7-8:  credible leadershiduct has a seat at the table
  5-6:  mixed signals — some strong, some weak
  3-4:  visible exec churn, contradictory public statements
  1-2:  leadership instability, founders departed

market_category (weight: 0.10)
  What to evaluate: Does this category have a 10+ year tailwind?
  9-10: massive and expanding (AI applications, fintech, dev tools)
  7-8:  healthy growth with real workflow pain to solve
  5-6:  mature but stable, horizontal productivity
  3-4:  commoditizing, low growth
  1-2:  shrinking category


=== ANCHOR COMPANIES ===

The anchor companies above define what each score means for each dimension.
When evaluating a new company, compare it against these anchors. If a company
listed as an anchor appears in the evaluation, its score MUST fall within the
stated band for that dimension.


=== CANDIDATE-SPECIFIC DISQUALIFIERS ===

If a company matches any of these categories, flag it — but do NOT let the
flag affect dimensional scores. Score every dimension on its own evidence.
Disqualifier ing is handled in code after scoring.

- Consultancy / agency — company's primary business is building software
  for other companies, not owning and operating its own products.

- Legacy enterprise sales org — large, established enterprise company where
  the sales organization drives product decisions and PM has no meaningful
  ownership (e.g., Oracle, SAP, Salesforce).

- Consumer social / gaming — company's primary business is consumer-facing
  social or gaming (e.g., Snap, Epic Games).


=== HARD CONSTRAINTS ===

Location: San Francisco Bay Area or Remote
Level: Senior PM, Staff PM, Principal PM, Group PM
Minimum total compensation: $250,000


=== ROLE EVALUATION CRITERIA ===

Title preferences (in order):
1. Senior Product Manager, Staff Product Manager (Growth)
2. Senior Product Manager (Monetization, Conversion, Activation)
3. Product Manager, Growth
4. Product Lead (if IC, not people management only)
5. Group Product Manager (if hands-on, not pure management)

Positive role signals:
- Growth,ization, or activation explicitly in scope
- Experimentation infrastructure or A/B testing mentioned
- Ownership of conversion funnel, onboarding, or trial optimization
- Cross-functional collaboration with data science and engineering
- Product-led growth motion (not purely sales-assisted)
- Small, high-leverage team with direct impact on revenue

Negative role signals:
- "Growth marketing" title with no product ownership
- Pure analytics or data science role without product authority
- Platform or infrastructure PM with no user-facing scope
- Roles where "growth" means headcount planning or team scaling
- Enterprise sales enablement disguised as product management
