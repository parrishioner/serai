SCORING DIMENSIONS
--------------------------------

1. role_interest (1–10)

How attractive this specific job is on its own merits, independent of company prestige.

This should reflect:
- ownership and decision-making leverage
- strategic importance of the area
- closeness to meaningful product problems
- likely day-to-day work
- whether the role feels energizing vs merely respectable

Ask:
“If this exact role existed at an average company, would it still stand out?”

Scoring:
- 9–10: exceptional
- 7–8: strong
- 5–6: acceptable
- 3–4: weak
- 1–2: low-interest

--------------------------------

2. strength_overlap (1–10)  **(MOST IMPORTANT SIGNAL FOR APPLY)**

Definition:
- Strength overlap measures how strongly the role’s core success depends on the candidate’s proven strengths.
- It is NOT about general plausibility, intelligence, or ability to learn.
- It is NOT about title similarity or company quality.

Candidate’s proven strengths:
- Derive these from the candidate profile, resume context, and anchor stories above
- Prioritize explicit, repeated evidence over inferred potential
- If a strength is not supported by those sections, treat it as unproven

Method (STRICT):
1. Identify the 3–5 MOST IMPORTANT capabilities required to succeed in this role
2. Focus on the TOP 2 most critical drivers
3. Compare those directly to the candidate’s proven strengths
4. Evaluate based on the role’s CENTER OF GRAVITY (not peripheral overlaps)
5. Penalize if the fit requires narrative stretching

Critical rules:
- Do NOT inflate score because the company is strong
- Do NOT inflate score because the role is senior
- Do NOT assume missing experience
- Do NOT reward adjacency or vague similarity
- Prefer direct, obvious, proven overlap

Narrative stretch penalty:
- If the reasoning sounds like “she could position her experience to fit,” this is NOT strong overlap
- Prefer clear, immediate credibility over inferred potential

Scoring:
- 9–10: role is directly built around multiple proven strengths; strong immediate leverage
- 7–8: meaningful overlap with core parts, but not full alignment
- 5–6: partial overlap; significant parts rely on less-proven areas
- 3–4: weak overlap; core success depends on missing strengths
- 1–2: very weak; requires major narrative stretch

--------------------------------

3. level_fit (1–10)

How well the role matches the candidate’s actual seniority and scope.

Consider:
- required experience
- leadership scope
- people management expectations
- org complexity
- domain/technical depth

Strict rule:
- Do NOT let level fit influence strength_overlap

Penalize:
- roles requiring management or domain prerequisites that the candidate profile does not explicitly support

Scoring:
- 9–10: excellent match
- 7–8: good match
- 5–6: stretch
- 3–4: mismatch
- 1–2: clear mismatch

Recommend the best next action for this role in isolation.

The system is optimized for speed:

Important:
- Be conservative with both Apply and Network
- Prefer Skip over Network when the role is merely plausible
- Network should be reserved for promising stretch cases or roles where extra effort could materially improve odds

Apply:
- Use only when the role appears clearly strong and likely worth immediate action
- Usually requires strong strength overlap, strong role interest, and solid level fit

Network:
- Use only when the role has real upside but is not strong enough for immediate Apply
- Best for good stretch roles, title mismatches, or high-value opportunities where a warm intro could make a difference

Skip:
- Use when the role is not strong enough to justify attention right now
- This should be common

Review:
- Use only if critical information is missing
